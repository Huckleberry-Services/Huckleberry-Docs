---
title: Equipment Graphics
description: Open a piece of equipment's own controls graphic from inside Huckleberry, and what has to be in place for it to work.
---

Every piece of equipment connected to a Niagara station can open its own
controls graphic &mdash; the same view your integrator built in the station
&mdash; without leaving Huckleberry and without logging into a separate system.

You&rsquo;ll find it on an equipment&rsquo;s detail page and in the Facility
Controls view. Where a graphic is available, the equipment carries a link to it;
opening the link displays the graphic over the page, titled with the equipment
it belongs to. Graphics can take a moment to load, and you&rsquo;ll see a notice
while that happens rather than a blank panel.

<Callout type="info">
Graphics show what the station shows. If your integrator built the view to allow
adjustments, you can make them here; if it&rsquo;s a read-only display, it stays
read-only. Huckleberry doesn&rsquo;t add or remove control from the graphic.
</Callout>

## What has to be in place

Five things have to line up. The first two are set up with you during
onboarding; the last three are usually in your station already, and are the ones
worth checking when a graphic doesn&rsquo;t appear.

### 1. A connected station

The site needs a Niagara station configured in Huckleberry, with its address and
credentials. This is the same connection that collects your equipment readings,
so if you&rsquo;re seeing live data from a site, the station is connected.

### 2. A graphics address, recorded separately

Huckleberry records two addresses for a station: the **station address** it
reads data from, and the **graphics address** &mdash; the landing page your Px
views are served from. They&rsquo;re kept apart on purpose.

<Callout type="warn">
Don&rsquo;t replace the station address with a link to a graphics page. They
look interchangeable and they aren&rsquo;t: the station address is what data
collection uses, and pointing it at a graphics page silently stops readings
coming in from that site. If you need the graphics location changed, ask us
rather than editing the station address.
</Callout>

### 3. Equipment linked to its device in the station

Huckleberry has to know which component in the station a piece of equipment
corresponds to. That link is made when your points are mapped. Equipment that
was created by hand and never mapped to the station has nothing to open, and
will say so.

### 4. A view assigned to the equipment in the station

This is the one that most often explains a missing graphic. Huckleberry links to
the **equipment&rsquo;s own component**, and Niagara displays whichever view is
assigned to that component. If your integrator assigned a Px view to the
component, it appears. If the graphic exists in the station but isn&rsquo;t
assigned to that component &mdash; it lives only in a folder, or it&rsquo;s
reachable from a menu page &mdash; there is nothing for the link to open.

Huckleberry verifies every link against the station before showing it, so an
equipment either gets a graphic that works or no link at all. You will never be
handed a link that lands on a station error page.

<Callout type="info">
If your integrator assigns a view later, you don&rsquo;t need to tell us.
Equipment with no graphic is re-checked against the station about once a day, so
new views are picked up on their own.
</Callout>

### 5. A reachable station

The station has to be online and reachable from Huckleberry at the moment you
open the graphic. A station that&rsquo;s down, or unreachable because of a
network or firewall change, will report that it didn&rsquo;t answer.

## Signing in to the station

Where your station is hosted on a Huckleberry address, opening a graphic signs
you in automatically &mdash; you go straight to the view.

Where your station is hosted on your own domain, your browser won&rsquo;t carry
a Huckleberry session across to it, so the graphic opens at the station&rsquo;s
own login page and you sign in with your station credentials. This is a
limitation of how browsers scope sessions between domains, not a configuration
mistake.

<Callout type="info">
Niagara allows one session per user account. If the station already has a
session open for your account &mdash; another tab, another machine, or a session
that didn&rsquo;t close cleanly &mdash; use **Reconnect** to sign out of the
station and start fresh. Reconnect signs you out of the station itself, not just
of Huckleberry, which is what clears a stuck session.
</Callout>

## What the messages mean

| What you see | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **No facility controller** | The client has no Niagara station configured. Nothing to open. |
| **Not linked to Niagara** | The station is connected, but this equipment isn&rsquo;t mapped to a device on it. |
| **No graphic found** | The equipment is mapped, but its component has no view assigned in the station. See requirement 4 above. |
| **Station unreachable** | The station didn&rsquo;t answer. It may be offline, or a network change may have cut the route. |
| **Unable to open a session** | The station answered but the sign-in didn&rsquo;t complete. Often a stuck session &mdash; try **Reconnect**. |

If a graphic is missing and the equipment is mapped, the station is up, and the
component has a view assigned, get in touch at
[info@huckleberryservices.com](mailto:info@huckleberryservices.com) and
we&rsquo;ll take a look.
