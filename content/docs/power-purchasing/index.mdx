Power Purchasing shows what each site pays for electricity, why it pays that,
and where buying or using power differently would save money. Huckleberry
models each utility account's cost from its tariff and the site's hourly load,
then checks that model against what you were actually billed. When the two
agree, you can trust the model to answer &ldquo;what if&rdquo; questions,
compare supply offers and forecast next year's spend.

Open **Power Purchasing** from the **Dashboards** menu. If you don't see it,
it isn't turned on for your account yet. Email
[info@huckleberryservices.com](mailto:info@huckleberryservices.com) and we'll
set it up.

<Callout type="info">
**Two inputs drive every number:** the tariff on each utility account and the
site's load shape. Confirm both on the **Rate &amp; load** tab first. The
What-if, supply and budget figures for a site are only as good as those two
inputs.
</Callout>

## Quick start

1. **Scan the portfolio** &mdash; the KPI strip and site table show where your
   electricity spend sits and which sites have weak data.
2. **Open a site** &mdash; check that every account has a matched rate.
3. **Confirm the rate and load** &mdash; on **Rate &amp; load**, pick the right
   tariff and the meter (or building type) that represents the site, then check
   that billed and modelled costs line up.
4. **Explore** &mdash; test operating changes on **What-if**, and compare
   supply offers on **Supply &amp; RTP**.
5. **Plan** &mdash; create next year's budget, approve it, and let Huckleberry
   track bills against it.

## The portfolio

The landing page summarises every site you have access to.

- **KPI strip** &mdash; effective rate (all-in $/kWh), load factor, peak
  demand, and the share of cost that comes from demand charges. Each figure is
  labelled with its source, such as *Billed* or *Modelled from tariff*.
- **Contract renewals** &mdash; supply contracts coming up for renewal and the
  date you need to act by.
- **Site table** &mdash; one row per site. **Data trust** and **Evidence** show
  how much of the site's model is measured (an interval meter, a confirmed
  tariff) and how much is estimated. Select a site to open it.

The buttons at the top right open [Price data](#price-data), the
[Contract calendar](#contract-calendar) and [Budget](#budget).

## A site

The site page repeats the portfolio KPIs for that site and lists its utility
accounts, each with its tariff and a rate status:

| Rate status | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Matched rate** | The tariff is set and its modelled cost lines up with the bills. |
| **Estimated rate** | Huckleberry's best guess. Confirm it on the Rate &amp; load tab. |
| **Needs a rate** | No tariff chosen yet, so the account can't be modelled. |
| **Custom rate** | A negotiated or special rate that can't be modelled from a published tariff. |

**Data trust** explains what lowers confidence in the site's numbers, such as
no interval meter, an unconfirmed tariff or missing bills, and what to fix
first. Work through the tabs from left to right: **Rate &amp; load**,
**What-if**, then **Supply &amp; RTP**.

### Delivery-only accounts

If a site buys its electricity from a competitive supplier, the utility only
delivers it. Once the supplier account is linked to the utility account on the
**Supply &amp; RTP** tab, Huckleberry prices the utility account as delivery
only: the tariff cost minus the utility's default supply, at its
price-to-compare. The account card notes this, so the billed and modelled
figures are compared like for like.

A delivery-only account needs the utility's price-to-compare, which you enter
in the supplier account's **Comparison settings** under **Price-to-compare
periods**. Until it's set, the account shows *Delivery only: needs the
utility's price-to-compare*.

## Rate &amp; load

How the site uses power, and what its tariff charges for it.

- **Typical day** &mdash; the site's hourly load shape by season. It comes from
  an interval meter when one is set as the load source. Otherwise it's a
  modelled profile for the building type, scaled to match the bills.
- **Load source** &mdash; pick the meter that measures the whole site, or, for a
  site that only has bills, choose its building type (office, school, retail,
  warehouse and so on).
- **Candidate rates** &mdash; for an account without a confirmed tariff,
  Huckleberry ranks the utility's tariffs by how well each one reproduces your
  bills. Pick the right one and select **Confirm this rate**.
- **Billed vs modelled** &mdash; each bill beside what the tariff model says it
  should have been. When the two lines agree, the rate and load are right.
- **Better-fit rate** &mdash; other tariffs the account could move to, and what
  each would cost on the same load.

<Callout type="warn">
**&ldquo;The tariff may be wrong&rdquo;** means billed and modelled costs differ
by more than this rate would explain. Common causes are the wrong tariff, a
rate change the bills don't reflect yet, or a load source that doesn't
represent the whole account. Check the candidate rates and the load source
before relying on the site's other numbers.
</Callout>

## What-if scenarios

Test operating changes before you commit to them. Every scenario is priced on
the site's own tariff and hourly load.

- **Shift load** &mdash; move energy from expensive hours into cheaper ones,
  such as pre-cooling a building in the morning. Set the share of load (or kWh
  per day) to shift, the hours to shift from and to, and the days and months it
  applies to.
- **Cut peak demand** &mdash; cap demand at a set kW, or cut each billing
  cycle's peak by a percentage. This is the lever for demand charges.
- **Annual effect** shows the change in cost by charge type, and the load chart
  shows the shape before and after.
- **Save** a scenario to compare options side by side or share them with your
  team.

## Supply &amp; RTP

For accounts that buy electricity from a competitive supplier: the contract,
how it's performing, and what to do at renewal.

- **Supplier and delivery links** pair the supplier's bill with the utility
  account that measures the kWh, so supplier cost can be checked against real
  usage. Linking also switches the utility account to
  [delivery-only pricing](#delivery-only-accounts).
- **Contract** &mdash; the price, term, notice period and what's included.
  **Contract checks** compare each supplier invoice with the contract and track
  volume against the swing band.
- **Capacity and transmission tags (PLC and NSPL)** drive a large share of the
  bill in PJM markets. Update them each June, when the new planning year starts.
- **Supply comparison** and **Renewal offers** price every offer on this site's
  own hourly load, so offers are compared like for like. **Comparison settings**
  hold the default-service details (customer class and price-to-compare
  periods) and the pass-through charges used for the comparison.
- **Download the RFP pack** to send your load data to suppliers.
- **Hedge ledger** tracks fixed-price blocks against the forecast and the
  approved budget. **Contract replay** re-prices past bills under the contract
  to show what it saved.
- **RTP comparison** shows what the site would pay on the utility's real-time
  price, using an RTP price series from [Price data](#price-data).

## Price data

The hourly market and utility prices used to price index offers, hedges and
real-time-price comparisons.

- **Add prices** uploads a CSV of hourly prices from your broker or utility.
  Index series price index-plus-adder offers and hedges; RTP series power the
  RTP comparison.
- **Coverage** shows the dates each series spans. Hours outside that range are
  reported as not fully priced.
- **Licence** controls whether a series can be shown in the browser and in
  PDFs, so licensed market data stays within its terms.
- Select a series to chart it.

## Contract calendar

Every supply contract's key dates for the next 24 months, so no contract rolls
over onto default rates unnoticed.

- **Act-by** is the date to start the renewal, a set number of days before the
  contract ends. **Notice** is the supplier's deadline for giving notice.
- Reminders go out ahead of each act-by date, at the lead times set on the
  contract (by default 60, 30 and 7 days before, and on the date itself).
- Select a site to open its **Supply &amp; RTP** tab and compare renewal offers
  before the act-by date.

## Budget

Build next year's electricity budget from the model, approve it, then track
actual bills against it month by month.

1. **New budget** &mdash; choose the sites to include. Each month starts from
   the billed cost for the same month last year, adjusted to normal weather, the
   rate's structure and the EIA price outlook. The budget shows the expected
   figure (P50) with a P10&ndash;P90 range: the range expected in 8 years out
   of 10.
2. **Review** &mdash; *Budget by month* and *By site* show which months and
   sites drive the total. Add notes to record your assumptions.
3. **Approve** &mdash; approving locks the budget in. **Budget vs actual** then
   follows each month's bills.
4. **Explain** &mdash; *What made the difference* splits each month's variance
   into weather, usage, rate and contract, so you can explain it.

Set the threshold for **Variance alerts** on the budget itself.

<Callout type="info">
Sites need recent electricity bills to be budgeted. A site whose accounts have
no bills, or no matched rate, is left out rather than budgeted at zero.
</Callout>

## Alerts

Turn Power Purchasing emails on or off in **Notification settings**, under
**Power Purchasing**. Both are checked once a day.

- **Budget variance** &mdash; an account's monthly cost is over or under its
  approved budget by more than the budget's threshold.
- **Contract renewals** &mdash; a supply contract reaches one of its act-by
  reminders.

## Troubleshooting

**An account says &ldquo;Needs a rate&rdquo;** &mdash; open the site's
**Rate &amp; load** tab and confirm one of the candidate rates. If none fits,
the account may buy supply from a competitive supplier: link the supplier on
**Supply &amp; RTP** so it's priced as delivery only.

**Billed and modelled don't line up** &mdash; check the tariff first, then the
load source. A meter that only covers part of the site, or a building type that
doesn't match how the site is used, will skew every month.

**&ldquo;Load is modelled, not metered&rdquo;** &mdash; the site has no
interval meter set as its load source, so its hourly shape comes from a
building profile. The monthly totals still match your bills; the hour-by-hour
shape is an estimate. Set an interval meter as the load source if you have one.

**&ldquo;Delivery only: needs the utility's price-to-compare&rdquo;** &mdash;
enter the price-to-compare periods in the supplier account's **Comparison
settings** on the **Supply &amp; RTP** tab.

**A site is missing from the budget** &mdash; it has no recent electricity
bills or no matched rate. Fix the rate or the bills, then recompute the budget.

**An offer or RTP comparison says it isn't fully priced** &mdash; the price
series doesn't cover every hour in the period. Upload the missing hours on
**Price data**.
