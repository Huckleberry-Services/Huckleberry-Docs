---
title: eGauge Meters
description: Connect the circuits on an eGauge meter, group them by category, and see them in your site's Equipment Energy Monitor, with history backfilled automatically.
---

Huckleberry can read interval data straight from an **eGauge** meter. Connect
the registers you care about (each measured circuit or feed) and Huckleberry
imports their history, then keeps polling for new readings. Electricity comes in
as kWh per interval plus average kW. Water comes in by volume and is shown in
gallons.

A guided setup walks you through the connection and shows you a sample of real
readings before anything is saved. You can set up one register at a time, or
tick several and set up a whole panel's circuits together.

<Callout type="info">
**Each register becomes its own meter.** When you set up several registers at
once, each still gets its own meter and its own connector, so you can rename,
pause, or retire any one of them later without touching the others.
</Callout>

## Before You Start

You'll need:

- **The meter's HTTPS address.** This is usually the eGauge proxy address,
  `https://<device-name>.egauge.io`, or the meter's own public HTTPS
  address. Addresses that only work inside your building's network can't be
  reached by Huckleberry.
- **A username and password** for the meter. A read-only account is enough.
- **The site the meter serves**, already set up in Huckleberry. If the site has
  a utility meter in Huckleberry, your circuits can be set up as its
  sub-meters (see [Sub-meters](#sub-meters)).

## Setting Up a Connector

Go to **Admin &rarr; Data Connectors**, choose **+ New connector**, select
**eGauge meter**, and click **Next**.

![The New connector dialog with the eGauge meter template](/images/integrations/egauge/01-new-connector.png)

### 1. Site

Pick the site the meter serves. Its readings are stored on meters at this
site.

![Choosing the site](/images/integrations/egauge/02-site.png)

### 2. Connect

Enter the meter's address, username, and password, then choose **Test
connection and discover registers**. Testing saves nothing. The credentials
are stored, encrypted, only when you activate the connector, and they're
never shown again.

![Entering the eGauge address and credentials](/images/integrations/egauge/03-connect.png)

If the test fails, the message tells you why. Common causes:

- **Wrong username or password.** Check the credentials on the meter.
- **Address not reachable.** Use the `https://` proxy address rather than a
  local network address.

### 3. Register

Huckleberry lists every register on the meter. Tick the ones to import. Tick
one register to set it up on its own, or several to set up a panel's circuits
together (see [Setting Up Several Circuits at Once](#setting-up-several-circuits-at-once)).

![Ticking registers to import](/images/integrations/egauge/04-register.png)

- Huckleberry decides what a register measures from its type on the meter, not
  from its name, so a register labelled "Water" that actually measures power is
  still treated as electricity.
- A register that's already imported by another connector is marked **Already
  connected**, with a link to that connector.
- A register that looks like the **panel's own total** (the meter's "use"
  register, or one named Main, Mains, Grid, Service, Total, or Usage) is marked
  as such. Leave it out when you're setting up the panel's circuits. If you do
  set it up, it's labelled **Panel total**, shown for reference, and never
  added to the circuits' totals, so nothing is counted twice.

The steps below describe setting up **one** register. With several ticked,
**Next** takes you to a single batch step instead.

### 4. Units

This step shows what the register measures and how its readings are stored.

![How the register's readings will be stored](/images/integrations/egauge/05-units.png)

- **Electricity** registers are stored as kWh per interval, plus average kW.
  There's nothing to confirm.
- **Volume** registers can measure water, gas, or other flows. Confirm that the
  register measures **water** to continue. Water is stored by volume and
  shown in gallons. Gas and other volume registers aren't supported yet.

### 5. Solar & Flow

Huckleberry suggests values based on the register. Check them, because they
decide how the meter counts in your totals.

![Choosing solar and flow](/images/integrations/egauge/06-solar-flow.png)

| The register is&hellip; | Solar | Flow |
|---|---|---|
| The grid feed at a site with solar panels | Yes | Net |
| A solar (PV) inverter | Yes | Generation |
| A generator | No | Generation |
| A load or a whole-building feed | No | Consumption |

A **Net** register measures what's drawn from the grid minus what's exported,
so some intervals can be negative. That's expected.

The solar question appears only for electricity.

### 6. Meter

Create a new meter at the site, or attach the register to an existing meter.
Only meters that match the utility, flow, and solar choice, and that don't
already receive readings from somewhere else, are offered.

![Naming the meter, choosing its parent and category](/images/integrations/egauge/07-meter.png)

- **Sub-meter of.** Choose the meter this circuit sits behind, usually the
  site's utility meter. Huckleberry pre-selects it when it can tell which one
  it is. A sub-meter's usage is already inside its parent, so site totals leave
  it out. Choose **Not a sub-meter** only if the circuit isn't behind any meter
  you have in Huckleberry. See [Sub-meters](#sub-meters).
- **Category.** The group this circuit belongs to in the site's
  [Equipment Energy Monitor](#equipment-energy-monitor), such as Chillers,
  Lighting, or Headhouse Climate. Huckleberry suggests one from the register's
  name; type your own or pick one you've used before. Leave it blank and the
  circuit shows under **Other**.
- Give the meter and the connector a name.

### 7. Preview and Activate

Choose the reading **interval** (1 minute, 5 minutes, 15 minutes, or 1 hour)
and how many **days of history** to backfill (up to 730).

Huckleberry runs a set of checks and reads a sample of real intervals from the
meter. Nothing is saved yet. The sample shows the total, the time zone the
meter reports in, how each value was calculated, and any gaps.

![The preview, with checks and sample readings](/images/integrations/egauge/08-preview.png)

<Callout type="info">
**Registers that count backwards are fixed for you.** Some eGauge installs
record a circuit's usage as a negative number. Huckleberry spots this from the
sample and flips the sign, so the circuit's readings come in as positive
usage. The preview shows **Sign** when it has done this, and so does the
connector's settings page afterwards.
</Callout>

When every check passes, choose **Activate**. Huckleberry starts importing the
history right away, and then polls for new readings on the interval you picked.

## Setting Up Several Circuits at Once

Tick two or more registers on the **Register** step and choose **Next**. One
batch step replaces the rest of the guided setup.

![Setting up three circuits from one panel together](/images/integrations/egauge/12-batch.png)

1. **Sub-meter of** applies to every electricity circuit that measures
   consumption. Huckleberry pre-selects the site's utility meter when it can.
   Generation, net, and water registers always stay standalone.
2. **Check each row.** Each register has its own solar and flow choice, a meter
   name, and a **category**, all pre-filled from the register. Water registers
   need you to confirm they measure water.
3. **Preview all** checks every row and reads a sample, one register at a time.
   Each row shows a tick and its last 24 hours of usage, or what went wrong.
4. **Activate all** activates the rows one at a time. If one fails, the others
   still go ahead; fix the problem and choose **Retry** on that row, or
   **Remove from batch** to leave it out.

When every row is activated, choose **Done**. Each circuit now has its own
connector under **Data Connectors**.

## Sub-meters

A circuit measured by an eGauge is usually already counted by the site's
utility meter. If both were added up, the same energy would be counted twice.
Marking the circuit as a **sub-meter** of the utility meter prevents that:

- **Site totals**, including **All Meters (total)** on the Facility Management
  page, count only the main meter.
- The circuit is still **shown on its own**, listed under its parent in the
  meter dropdown, so you can look at it individually.

You can set or change a circuit's parent at any time on its connector's
settings page.

## Equipment Energy Monitor

Sites with eGauge circuits get an **Equipment Energy Monitor** on their
**Facility Management** page. It shows where the site's energy goes, grouped by
the categories you gave your circuits.

![The Equipment Energy Monitor on a site's Facility Management page](/images/integrations/egauge/13-energy-monitor.png)

- **Periods:** Today, Yesterday, Last 7 Days, Last 30 Days, or a custom range of
  up to 400 days.
- **kWh or kW:** energy per interval, or average demand.
- **Category chips:** click a category to hide or show it in the chart and the
  table.
- **The chart** draws one line per category. A **Panel total** is drawn as a
  dashed reference line, so you can see how much of the panel your circuits
  account for.
- **Summary by Category** lists each category's usage for today, yesterday, the
  last 7 days, and the last 30 days. Click a row to see its circuits. A circuit
  that has stopped reporting shows **N/A** and is marked **offline**.
- **Download CSV** exports the table, one line per circuit plus a subtotal per
  category.

Times are shown in the site's own time zone. The monitor covers the whole site,
even when you're looking at one building.

## Watching the Import

Open the connector from **Data Connectors** to see how the import is going.

![Health and progress for an active eGauge connector](/images/integrations/egauge/09-health.png)

- **Health and progress** shows the connector's status, the last sync, and
  any failures in a row.
- **Backfill and data** shows how far back history goes, how much has been
  imported, and the latest reading stored. The bar fills as the backfill
  runs. A long history is imported over several runs.
- **Gaps** are periods where the meter had no data. **Dropped resets and
  spikes** are intervals Huckleberry left out because the meter's counter
  reset or jumped implausibly. Leaving them out keeps your totals accurate.
- **Source** shows the meter's details, including its clock. A large clock
  skew can make readings land in the wrong interval.

Use **Sync now** to run an import immediately, and **Pause** to stop polling
for a while.

## Changing a Connector

Some settings can be changed at any time: the connector's name, the flow, the
solar choice, the **sub-meter** parent, the **category**, and how many days of
history to backfill.

![Fixed setup and the settings you can change](/images/integrations/egauge/10-fixed-setup.png)

- **A longer backfill** reads further back. New readings keep arriving while
  the older history fills in over the next few runs, so your current data never
  pauses.
- **A shorter backfill** stops reading older history. If older history was
  still being filled in, Huckleberry stops at the new start.
- **Category and sub-meter** changes apply straight away to the Equipment
  Energy Monitor and to site totals.

The site, meter, register, utility, and interval are fixed, because changing
them would mix readings from different sources. To change any of them, retire
the connector and run the guided setup again.

**Updating credentials.** If the meter's password changes, enter the new
username and password under **Credentials**. They're checked against the same
meter before they replace the old ones.

![Updating credentials and retiring a connector](/images/integrations/egauge/11-credentials-retire.png)

**Retiring a connector** stops its imports permanently and frees the register.
The meter and all of its readings are kept. Retiring can't be undone. To
import the same register again, run the guided setup, which reuses the same
meter.

## How eGauge Readings Show Up

- **Electricity** meters appear with your site's other meters and count toward
  energy totals, charts, and exports according to the flow you chose. A
  **sub-meter** is shown on its own but left out of site totals, because its
  parent already counts it.
- **Circuits with a category** are grouped in the site's Equipment Energy
  Monitor. A **Panel total** is shown there for reference and never added up.
- **Solar** meters are counted as solar generation and are kept separate from
  the energy your buildings use.
- **Water** meters are shown in gallons and are never included in electricity
  totals, kWh charts, or energy exports.
